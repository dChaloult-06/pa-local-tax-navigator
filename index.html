Act 32 & Local Earned Income Tax (EIT)

Core Framework
⚙What Act 32 Does

Act 32 of 2008 consolidated Pennsylvania's local EIT collection from 560+ separate collectors into 69 Tax Collection Districts (TCDs), effective January 1, 2012. It standardized withholding rules, forms, and remittance destinations statewide — with two notable exceptions: Philadelphia and Pittsburgh operate under their own frameworks.

The "Higher-Of" Withholding Rule — Act 32 Core Mechanic

For every Pennsylvania employee, compare the two rates and withhold the higher one:

Withhold = MAX( Total Resident EIT Rate, Work Location Non-Resident EIT Rate )

Remit to the tax collector for the work location TCD. The collector distributes the resident's share to their home TCD.

Employer Setup Checklist

1

Collect Residency Certification Form

Required at hire and on any address change. Captures home and work PSD codes and EIT rates.

2

Look up both PSD codes

Use the DCED Address Search for home and work addresses.

3

Compare rates — withhold the higher

Apply the "higher-of" rule. If rates are equal, withhold the resident tax.

4

Register with work-location TCD collector

Register with each collector for your PA worksites. Multi-site employers may elect combined monthly filing.

5

Remit quarterly (or monthly for combined filers)

EIT due 30 days after quarter-end. Combined filers remit monthly.

Local Services Tax (LST)

Work-Location Tax · Separate from Act 32
ℹ LST and Act 32 Are Separate Systems

The Local Services Tax is governed by its own statute and has its own withholding, collection, and exemption rules. Act 32 governs EIT only. Employers must handle both independently — a change in EIT setup does not automatically affect LST, and vice versa.

Financially distressed municipalities authorized under special legislation may charge more than the standard $52 cap. Always verify the actual LST amount via the DCED Address Search for the specific work location — do not assume $52 applies universally.

🛡LST Exemptions & Special Cases

Low-Income Exemption

⚠ Mandatory When LST > $10/Year

Employees whose total earned income from all sources within the taxing jurisdiction is less than $12,000 annually must be exempted. Some jurisdictions set this threshold at $15,600 — verify with the local tax collector.

  • Employer must make LST-E form available to employees
  • Employee completes and files with employer — do not start withholding until form is reviewed
  • Employer must stop withholding upon receipt of valid LST-E
  • Withholding must resume if employee notifies eligibility has ended or collector instructs

ℹ Mid-Year Hires

LST is only owed for payroll periods in which the employee is actually employed. Pro-rate the annual LST from the hire date — do not withhold the full annual amount from a late-year hire.

ℹ Veteran Exemption

Veterans with a 100% permanent service-connected disability declared by the U.S. Veterans Administration, or any person serving as a member of a reserve component called to active duty during the tax year, are exempt from LST. Retain documentation.

Philadelphia Wage Tax

Special Rule — Act 32 Does Not Apply
🔴 Philadelphia Is Exempt from Act 32

Philadelphia operates its own Wage Tax system independently through the Philadelphia Department of Revenue. PSD codes starting with 51 trigger Philadelphia rules — do not apply standard Act 32 EIT logic. Philadelphia also has no reciprocal tax agreement with any other state or municipality.

Philadelphia City Council approves annual rate changes effective July 1. Rates are on a planned multi-year reduction schedule through 2030 (targeting 3.70% resident / 3.39% non-resident). Always verify current rates at the Philadelphia Department of Revenue before configuring payroll.

⚠ Act 48 — Philly Residents Working Elsewhere in PA

Act 48 of 1994 requires ALL Pennsylvania employers — even those outside Philadelphia — to withhold the Philadelphia resident Wage Tax (3.74%) for employees who live in Philadelphia. This applies regardless of where the worksite is located within PA.

🏠Remote Work & Philadelphia — Convenience of Employer Doctrine
🔴 Philadelphia Applies a Convenience-of-Employer Rule

For non-residents, the Philadelphia Wage Tax applies to compensation for work performed in Philadelphia and for work performed outside Philadelphia at the employee's own convenience. If an employer requires an employee to work outside Philadelphia, those wages are not subject to the Wage Tax. But if the employee chooses to work remotely, Philadelphia still claims those wages. This creates significant risk for remote-work arrangements involving Philadelphia-based employers or Philadelphia-resident employees.

Recommendation: Document whether remote work is employer-directed or employee-elected. Consult the Philadelphia Dept. of Revenue or a qualified tax advisor for complex remote arrangements.

ℹ Employee Advisory — For Communication to Employees

The credits above (NJ-COJ, MD Form 502CR) are personal tax return actions the employee takes at year-end — they are not employer withholding adjustments. Pittsburgh Special Rules

Allegheny County · PSD Codes 02xxxx
⚠ Pittsburgh Has Its Own Filing and Collection Structure

Pittsburgh-area employers file EIT through Allegheny County's four Tax Collection Districts — not the statewide standard TCD structure. LST in Pittsburgh is split between city and school district, and the City of Pittsburgh has its own LST forms and portal. Additionally, Pittsburgh employers are subject to a Payroll Expense Tax that has no equivalent elsewhere in PA.

ℹ Payroll Expense Tax — Employer Obligation, Not Employee Withholding

The Pittsburgh Payroll Expense Tax (0.55%, expressed as 5.5 mills) is assessed on the employer — it is not withheld from employee wages and does not appear on employee paystubs. It applies to total gross taxable compensation paid to employees performing work or rendering services within Pittsburgh city limits. This is separate from and in addition to employee EIT withholding obligations. Register and file through the Pittsburgh Department of Finance.

🔗Pittsburgh Resources & Filing

Pittsburgh and Philadelphia are the only PA jurisdictions where EIT remittance does not go through the standard TCD collector structure. Pittsburgh has its own tax forms and submission portal through the City's Department of Finance.

📋City of Pittsburgh — Dept. of Finance · Official SourcePittsburgh Tax Information — LST, EIT & Payroll Expense Tax
⚠ Act 205 — City of York Parallel

The City of York (York County) also has an elevated EIT rate of 1.25% (city + school) under Act 205 of 1984 for distressed pension funding — separate from Pittsburgh's rules. The 1.25% applies to workers in York City specifically, not all of York County. Non-residents of York City working there pay at least 1.25%. Verify via the DCED Address Search.

Out-of-State Employees & Cross-Border Rules

880000 Code · PA–MD Reciprocity · Courtesy Withholding
ℹ The Core Principle: State Reciprocity ≠ Local Tax Exemption

Pennsylvania has state-level reciprocal agreements with Indiana, Maryland, New Jersey, Ohio, Virginia, and West Virginia. These agreements apply to state income tax on W-2 wages only. They do not automatically exempt employees from PA local EIT or LST. Local tax rules are governed separately and must be evaluated independently.

Scenario 1 — Employee Lives Out of State, Works at a PA Worksite

This scenario is defined by DCED official guidance. This includes NJ or MD residents commuting to a Pennsylvania office or job site.

DCED Official Rule — The 880000 Code

For any employee who lives outside Pennsylvania, the employer must:
Set Resident PSD Code = 880000  |  Set Total Resident EIT Rate = 0%
The out-of-state resident still owes and must have withheld: the Work Location Non-Resident EIT Rate and the LST for the PA worksite municipality.

NJ Special Rule — Withholding Offset

ℹ NJ Setup Note — PA Local EIT Reduces NJ State Withholding

For New Jersey residents working in Pennsylvania, any PA local EIT withheld reduces the amount of NJ state income tax that should be withheld from the same wages. This is a payroll setup configuration decision — confirm how your payroll system handles this offset. The employee may also claim a Schedule NJ-COJ credit on their personal NJ return for PA local taxes paid.

ℹ Employee Advisory — NJ & MD Residents

Employees living in NJ or MD who have PA local EIT withheld may be eligible for a credit on their home state personal return. This is the employee's personal return action — not an employer withholding adjustment:
NJ residents: Schedule NJ-COJ (filed with NJ-1040)
MD residents: Form 502CR, Part A, Line 15 (filed with MD-502)

Scenario 2 — PA Resident Working for an Out-of-State Employer

This scenario covers remote workers and commuters whose employer has no physical presence in Pennsylvania.

DCED Official Rule — Out-of-State Employers

Out-of-state employers are NOT required to complete the PA Residency Certification Form or withhold PA local EIT for Pennsylvania resident employees. The PA resident employee remains legally responsible for their home municipality's EIT regardless of where their employer is located.

✓If the Employer Chooses Courtesy Withholding
1

Withhold the Total Resident EIT Rate

Use the employee's PA home municipality rate (from DCED Address Search).

2

Set work-side PSD to 880000

For reporting purposes, the work location is out-of-state. Use code 880000 on the work side of the Residency Certificate.

3

Remit to the employee's home TCD collector

Out-of-state employers may remit all PA local EIT to a single PA Tax Officer, who distributes to the correct resident collector.

⚠If the Employer Does NOT Withhold
🔴 Employee Must Make Quarterly Estimated Payments

The PA resident employee is still legally obligated to pay their home municipality's EIT. Without employer withholding, the employee must:

  • Calculate their Total Resident EIT Rate via DCED Address Search
  • Make quarterly estimated payments directly to their home TCD tax collector
  • File an annual local EIT return by April 15
  • Failure to pay results in interest (PA Fiscal Code rate) and a 1% penalty per month (up to 15%)
⚠ Employer Best Practice

Even when not legally required, DCED and local collectors recommend out-of-state employers provide courtesy withholding. It reduces employee compliance burden and reduces the employer's risk of being held responsible if the employee fails to pay.

⚠ Maryland Form 515 — County Local Tax Trigger

A PA resident working in Maryland may be subject to Maryland county local income tax — but only if their PA home municipality imposes an EIT on Maryland residents working in PA.

Exception: PA residents living in York or Adams County are generally exempt from MD county local tax, because those TCDs do not levy EIT on Maryland residents — so Maryland does not reciprocate.

The MD employer withholds county local tax when applicable and the employee files Maryland Form 515.

PA–Maryland Local Tax Exemption — Deep Dive

This section covers one of the most complex and frequently misunderstood areas of PA local tax: the TCD-level local reciprocity between Pennsylvania and Maryland.

How the PA–MD Local Exemption Works

The PA-MD state reciprocity agreement covers state income tax only. However, certain Pennsylvania Tax Collection Districts (TCDs) have separately agreed — through legal settlements or local ordinance — to exempt Maryland residents from local nonresident EIT withholding. This is a TCD-by-TCD determination, not a blanket statewide rule.

Critical: LST is NEVER covered by any PA–MD local exemption. LST applies to all workers at a PA worksite regardless of residency or TCD exemption status.

✅ York & Adams County — Confirmed Exemption

In 1981, a legal settlement was reached between certain York Adams Tax Bureau member taxing authorities and the State of Maryland. The settlement requires Maryland residents employed in York or Adams County to file and pay local tax only to Maryland. Pennsylvania employers in these counties are not required to withhold PA local EIT from Maryland residents.

  • Applies to EIT only — LST still applies
  • MD residents only — does not apply to other states
  • York or Adams County worksites only — does not apply to other PA counties
  • If EIT was withheld in error, employee is entitled to a full refund from the TCD
🔗York Adams Tax Bureau — Official SourceMaryland Resident Exemption Documentation
⚠ Other TCD Exemptions — Must Verify

Beyond York and Adams, a broader set of PA Tax Collection Districts may also exempt Maryland residents from nonresident EIT. This list is maintained by individual TCDs and reflected in payroll tax calculation guides used by enterprise payroll software — it is not publicly enumerated on the DCED website.

How to verify for your specific worksite:

  • Contact the work-location TCD collector directly and ask whether they honor the Maryland resident EIT exemption
  • Review your payroll system's PA local tax configuration guide for an "MD Exempt" flag by TCD
  • If your system suppresses PA EIT for a Maryland employee — that may be correct configuration, not an error

ℹ Employee Advisory — MD Form 502CR Credit

When PA local EIT is withheld from a Maryland resident's wages, the employee may claim a credit on their Maryland resident income tax return using Form 502CR, Part A, Line 15. This is a personal return action — not an employer withholding adjustment. The credit offsets Maryland state and/or local tax liability on the same income. Employees should consult their personal tax advisor or the Maryland Comptroller's guidance.
