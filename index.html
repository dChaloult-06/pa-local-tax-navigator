Payroll Setup Directive

Core Framework

Act 32 & Local Earned Income Tax (EIT)

Act 32 of 2008 consolidated Pennsylvania's local EIT collection into 69 Tax Collection Districts, effective January 1, 2012. Philadelphia and Pittsburgh operate under separate frameworks.

The "Higher-Of" Withholding Rule

For every Pennsylvania employee, compare the two rates and withhold the higher:

Withhold = MAX( Total Resident EIT Rate, Work Location Non-Resident EIT Rate )

Remit to the collector for the work location TCD. The collector distributes the resident portion to the employee's home TCD.

Employer Setup Checklist
1

2

Use the DCED Address Search. Use the Lookup Tool in this navigator to generate a complete setup directive.

3

Apply the withholding rate

The DCED results show "Total Taxes to be Withheld" — this is already the Higher-Of result. Use that rate directly.

4

See the section for contact and portal links.

5

Remit quarterly

EIT due 30 days after quarter-end. Multi-site employers may elect monthly combined filing with a single collector.

Work-Location Tax · Separate from Act 32

Local Services Tax (LST)

The LST is a flat annual fee for the privilege of working in a jurisdiction, governed by its own statute — separate from Act 32 — with its own withholding, collection, and exemption rules.

LST and Act 32 are completely separate systems

A change in EIT setup does not affect LST, and vice versa. LST is based on work location only — not the employee's residence.

LST Exemptions and Special Cases

Low-Income Exemption

Mandatory when LST exceeds $10/year

Employees whose total earned income within the taxing jurisdiction is below $12,000 annually must be exempted. This is the statutory minimum threshold — verify the applicable amount with the local collector for your specific jurisdiction.

  • Make Form LST-E available to employees at hire and upon request
  • Employee completes and returns before withholding stops
  • Stop withholding upon receipt of a valid LST-E
  • Resume withholding if the employee notifies you their eligibility has ended

Special Rule — Act 32 Does Not Apply

Philadelphia Wage Tax

Philadelphia operates its own Wage Tax system through the Philadelphia Department of Revenue. It has no reciprocal tax agreement with any state or municipality.

Philadelphia is exempt from Act 32

PSD codes starting with 51 trigger Philadelphia rules. Do not apply standard Act 32 EIT logic.

Act 48 — Philly residents working elsewhere in PA

All PA employers — including those outside Philadelphia — must withhold the Philadelphia resident Wage Tax (3.74%, Eff. July 1, 2025) for employees who live in Philadelphia, regardless of where the worksite is located within PA.

Remote Work — Convenience-of-Employer Doctrine
Philadelphia applies a convenience-of-employer rule

For non-residents, Philadelphia Wage Tax applies to work performed in the city and to work performed outside the city at the employee's own convenience. If an employer requires work outside Philadelphia, those wages are not subject to the tax. If the employee chooses to work remotely, Philadelphia still claims those wages. Document whether remote work is employer-directed or employee-elected.

Allegheny County · PSD Codes 02xxxx

Pittsburgh Special Rules

Pittsburgh has its own EIT filing structure, LST split, and an employer-side Payroll Expense Tax with no equivalent elsewhere in PA. EIT is collected by Jordan Tax Service.

Pittsburgh has its own filing and collection structure

Pittsburgh-area employers file EIT through Allegheny County's four Tax Collection Districts. The City of Pittsburgh has its own LST forms and portal through the Department of Finance.

Key Notes
Payroll Expense Tax — employer obligation only

The 0.55% Payroll Expense Tax is assessed on the employer. It is not withheld from employee wages and does not appear on employee pay stubs. It applies to total gross taxable compensation for employees performing work within Pittsburgh city limits — separate from and in addition to employee EIT withholding.

Allegheny County — four TCDs

Unlike most PA counties, Allegheny County has four separate Tax Collection Districts. Verify which TCD your Pittsburgh-area worksite falls under via the DCED Address Search.

880000 Code · PA–MD Reciprocity · Courtesy Withholding

Out-of-State Employees and Cross-Border Rules

State reciprocity agreements apply to state income tax only. They do not automatically exempt employees from PA local EIT or LST. Local rules must be evaluated independently.

State reciprocity does not equal local tax exemption

PA has state-level reciprocal agreements with Indiana, Maryland, New Jersey, Ohio, Virginia, and West Virginia — for state income tax only. PA local EIT and LST obligations are governed separately.

DCED Official Rule — The 880000 Code

For any employee who lives outside Pennsylvania: set Resident PSD Code = 880000 and Total Resident EIT Rate = 0%. The out-of-state resident still owes the Work Location Non-Resident EIT Rate and LST for the PA worksite municipality.

NJ setup note — PA local EIT may reduce NJ state withholding

For NJ residents working in PA, any PA local EIT withheld may reduce the NJ state income tax that should be withheld from the same wages. Confirm how your payroll system handles this offset. The employee may also claim Schedule NJ-COJ on their personal NJ return for PA local taxes paid.

Employee advisory — NJ and MD residents

Employees in NJ or MD who have PA local EIT withheld may claim a credit on their home-state personal return. This is a personal return action — not an employer withholding adjustment.
NJ residents: Schedule NJ-COJ (filed with NJ-1040)
MD residents: Form 502CR, Part A (filed with MD-502)

DCED Official Rule — Out-of-State Employers

Out-of-state employers are not required to withhold PA local EIT for PA resident employees. The PA resident employee remains legally responsible for their home municipality's EIT regardless of where the employer is located.

If employer chooses courtesy withholding

1

Withhold Total Resident EIT Rate

Use the employee's PA home municipality rate from the DCED Address Search.

2

Set work-side PSD to 880000

The work location is out-of-state. Use 880000 on the work side of the Residency Certificate.

3

Remit to employee's home TCD collector

Out-of-state employers may remit all PA local EIT to a single PA Tax Officer for distribution to the correct resident collector.

If employer does not withhold

Employee must make quarterly estimated payments

The PA resident is still legally obligated to pay their home municipality's EIT. Without employer withholding, the employee must:

  • Calculate their Total Resident EIT Rate via DCED Address Search
  • Make quarterly estimated payments to their home TCD collector
  • File an annual local EIT return by April 15
  • Failure results in interest and a 1% monthly penalty (up to 15%)

How the PA–MD Local Exemption Works

The PA-MD state reciprocity agreement covers state income tax only. However, certain Pennsylvania TCDs have agreed — through legal settlements or local ordinance — to exempt Maryland residents from local nonresident EIT withholding. This is determined TCD by TCD, not statewide.

LST is never covered by any PA–MD local exemption. LST applies to all workers at a PA worksite regardless of TCD exemption status.

York and Adams County — confirmed exemption

In 1981, a legal settlement was reached between York Adams Tax Bureau member authorities and the State of Maryland. Maryland residents employed in York or Adams County file and pay local tax only to Maryland.

  • EIT only — LST still applies at the work location rate
  • Maryland residents only — does not apply to other states
  • If EIT was withheld in error, the employee is entitled to a full refund from the TCD
Other TCD exemptions — must verify

Beyond York and Adams, other PA TCDs may also exempt Maryland residents from nonresident EIT. This is maintained at the TCD level and may be reflected in your payroll system's configuration.

How to verify: Contact the work-location TCD collector directly and ask whether they honor the Maryland resident EIT exemption. If your payroll system suppresses PA EIT for a Maryland employee, that may be correct configuration — not an error.
